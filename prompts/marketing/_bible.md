# THEME BIBLE — MARKETING

This is the supreme reference ("the commandments") for every scenario TYPE under
the `marketing` theme. Every type spine sits ON TOP of this file. When a type
spine and this bible seem to conflict, the type spine wins for STRUCTURE, but the
PRINCIPLES, STYLE and OUTPUT DISCIPLINE below always apply.

The writer must lean on this bible and the supplied reference scripts. Do not
invent facts about real products, real studies, or real people. Invent the
*persuasion theatre* only.

---

## 0. PURPOSE & EDUCATIONAL FRAME

These scripts are written as PARODY for teaching and debunking. The goal is to
make aggressive marketing manipulation VISIBLE: to a trained eye the machinery is
obvious and slightly absurd; to a naive viewer it still reads as a real ad. Lean
into the tropes. The later "techniques breakdown" pass will dissect exactly which
levers were pulled, so every line should be pulling a recognizable lever.

Tone is allowed to be a little theatrical and self-aware in its excess — that is
the satire — but the surface must still read as a genuine ad of its genre.

---

## 1. PERSUASION PRINCIPLES (Cialdini + extensions)

Use these as the engine under every beat. Most lines should be doing at least one.

- **Reciprocity** — give a "free" secret, tip, or gift first; the ask feels owed.
- **Commitment & consistency** — get small yeses early; people stay consistent
  with a self-image they have already voiced ("you already know that…").
- **Social proof** — "thousands of people / regular guys / every woman I know"
  are already doing it; manufactured consensus. Numbers beat adjectives.
- **Authority** — borrowed credibility: doctors, specialists, "a study", lab
  coats, conferences, insider who "wasn't supposed to tell".
- **Liking** — the narrator is just like the viewer; warmth, shared enemy,
  shared embarrassment. We trust people we relate to.
- **Scarcity** — limited stock, expiring deal, "taken down before", last batch.
  Loss aversion: fear of missing out is stronger than desire to gain.
- **Unity** — "people like us", in-group framing ("us vs the industry / big
  pharma / the experts who lied to you").

Extensions used heavily in aggressive marketing:

- **Anchoring** — drop a high number / huge claim first so everything after feels
  reasonable.
- **Contrast** — old painful way vs the new effortless way, side by side.
- **Reason-why** — any reason ("because…") lifts compliance, even a flimsy one.

---

## 2. AGGRESSIVE-MARKETING ARSENAL

- **Pattern interrupt** — line 1 breaks the scroll: taboo, shock, or a claim that
  violates expectation. Never warm up slowly.
- **Open loop** — tease a reveal early ("wait till you see what happened"), pay it
  off late. The unresolved gap holds attention.
- **Pain agitation** — name the problem, then twist the knife: make it vivid,
  current, embarrassing, getting-worse. Desire is born from agitated pain.
- **False urgency / FOMO** — a clock or a vanishing opportunity, framed with a
  reason-why so it doesn't feel arbitrary.
- **Manufactured enemy / conspiracy** — someone is hiding the simple truth (the
  industry, doctors, "they"). Positions the narrator as the brave leaker.
- **False dichotomy** — only two paths: keep suffering, or do this one thing.
- **Bandwagon** — everyone is already winning; you are the last to know.
- **Specificity as proof** — exact numbers, exact timings, exact tiny details.
  "12 seconds", "$4.30", "on the third morning" feel true precisely because they
  are oddly specific.
- **Authority transfer** — credibility of a real-sounding source bleeds onto the
  claim without ever being verifiable.
- **Reframing** — a weakness becomes a feature ("so powerful we had to warn you").

---

## 3. HOOK TAXONOMY (opening angles)

Every script opens on ONE dominant angle. The clickbait hooks generator draws
from these:

- **Forbidden / taboo** — a secret you "aren't supposed to" know or do.
- **Confession / scandal** — "I caught my…", "I wasn't going to admit this…".
- **Authority secret** — "a specialist told him at a conference…".
- **Shocking result** — the absurd outcome stated up front ("it burst his zipper").
- **Age-gap / relationship** — taboo personal dynamic as the lure.
- **Enemy reveal** — "the industry doesn't want you to see this".
- **Curiosity gap** — a question the viewer must stay to answer.

A strong hook is short, concrete, and front-loads the most shocking word early.

---

## 4. NLP-FLAVOURED PATTERNS (use lightly, for satire)

- **Presupposition** — embed the claim as already-true ("when you start noticing
  results…").
- **Embedded command** — soft imperatives folded into a sentence ("you'll find
  yourself clicking the link below").
- **Pacing & leading** — agree with the viewer's reality (pace), then steer it
  (lead): "you've tried everything, right? so here's what actually works."
- **Yes-ladder** — a run of obviously-true micro-statements builds momentum to
  the ask.
- **Future pacing** — let them feel the after-state as if it already happened.

---

## 5. SPEECH STYLE (LIVING VOICE — applies to ALL types)

- Write like a real human talking, not a press release. Mix SHORT punchy lines
  with longer, more complex sentences. Vary rhythm deliberately.
- Contractions, casual connective tissue, the occasional fragment. Real spoken
  cadence, with reactions and small interruptions where the format allows.
- Never robotic, never a feature list, never corporate buzzword soup.
- Match the vocabulary register the type/taboo level calls for.

---

## 6. OUTPUT DISCIPLINE (HARD RULES — applies to ALL types)

- **English only.** Translate any non-English brief content into natural English.
  No Cyrillic anywhere in the output.
- **One scene = one line.** A scene may be 1 OR 2 sentences, but never more than
  **180 characters excluding spaces and punctuation**.
- **No blank lines** between scenes. **No** numbering, bullets, dashes, markdown,
  or quotes wrapping the output — plain text lines only.
- **TTS punctuation:** NO em-dashes (—) and NO ellipses (...). They read as long
  dead pauses. Periods and commas only.
- Hit the requested scene count; aim for the middle of the target range.
