TYPE: Dialogue / Interview — a staged two-person interview where the product or
method surfaces through "candid" conversation.

Obey the THEME BIBLE above. The core device of this genre is the STAGED INTERVIEW
itself: it looks spontaneous and journalistic, but every question is engineered
to tee up a sales point, and every answer sells. Make it feel real while the
machinery does its work.

EDUCATIONAL FRAME: teaching parody of the "fake candid interview / planted
testimonial" format — showcase how leading questions, the interviewer-as-proxy,
and the reluctant reveal manipulate trust, so it can be dissected later.

CAST (exactly 2):
- INTERVIEWER — plays the viewer's proxy: curious, lightly skeptical, asks the
  questions the audience is thinking, and steers toward the payoff.
- GUEST — the authority / success story / expert. Delivers the mechanism, the
  proof, and the reveal.

STAGED-INTERVIEW DEVICES (weave in, per the bible):
- Leading questions that pre-frame the answer the way the sale needs it.
- Interviewer-as-skeptic: voices the doubt, gets it dissolved on camera
  (controlled objection handling).
- Reluctant reveal: the guest hesitates before "sharing the secret" — scarcity of
  knowledge, builds intrigue (open loop).
- Manufactured spontaneity: reactions, small interruptions, "wait, really?"
- Authority + social proof: credentials, "the people I've helped".

STRUCTURE (follow IN ORDER, blended across the chosen number of scenes):
1. Cold intro — interviewer frames who the guest is and why it matters
2. The hook question — the thing the viewer is wondering
3. Reluctant-reveal setup — the guest hesitates, intrigue builds
4. The mechanism / backstory — how and why it works
5. Interviewer pushes back (skeptic) — guest overcomes with proof
6. Concrete proof / demonstration / before-after, told in dialogue
7. Social proof — others who tried it
8. Scarcity / why-now
9. Soft-but-clear CTA — interviewer points the viewer to the link

OUTPUT TAGGING (NON-NEGOTIABLE):

  [VO] only if the voiceover option is enabled. After the tag — only the clean
  spoken words. One beat per line.
- Use ONLY the two cast names (and [VO] when enabled). Never invent speakers.

SCENE/LINE RULES:
- One beat per line. 1-2 sentences, max 180 characters excluding spaces and
  punctuation.
- Living speech: mix short and longer lines, real back-and-forth rhythm,
  interruptions and reactions. Never robotic.
